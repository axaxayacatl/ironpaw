THE NINE LIVES TIMELINE

FELINE DEVELOPMENT / AGE CLASSIFICATION / COMPANION CARE

SELECT A LIFE STAGE TO EXPAND ITS DEVELOPMENT PROFILE AND AGE-APPROPRIATE ENRICHMENT RECOMMENDATIONS.

LIFE 01 / ORIGIN
NEONATAL
0–2 WEEKS
Newborn kittens are dependent on their mother or qualified neonatal caregivers for warmth, feeding, elimination, and care. DEVELOPMENT Eyes and ears are developing. Movement is limited. ENRICHMENT PROFILE Stable warmth, appropriate feeding, gentle handling when appropriate, and a clean, quiet environment. CARE PRIORITY Monitor feeding, warmth, weight gain, and elimination. Neonatal kittens require specialized care.
LIFE 02 / DISCOVERY
EARLY KITTENHOOD
2–7 WEEKS
Kittens begin exploring their environment and developing coordination and social behaviors. DEVELOPMENT Walking, sensory exploration, play, and early social learning. ENRICHMENT PROFILE Safe textures, gentle exploration, age-appropriate toys, and positive human interaction. CARE PRIORITY Maintain maternal care when possible and arrange veterinary guidance for development and feeding.
LIFE 03 / EXPLORATION
LATE KITTENHOOD
7–12 WEEKS
Curiosity and independent exploration become increasingly prominent. DEVELOPMENT Coordination, social play, environmental learning. ENRICHMENT PROFILE Supervised toy play, hiding spaces, simple food puzzles appropriate to developmental ability, and exploration. CARE PRIORITY Support socialization, veterinary care, and safe transitions.
LIFE 04 / EXPANSION
JUVENILE
3–6 MONTHS
Growing kittens develop stronger movement skills and increasingly complex play behaviors. DEVELOPMENT Coordination, climbing, chasing, and independent discovery. ENRICHMENT PROFILE Interactive wand toys, tunnels, climbing opportunities, supervised exploration, and beginner puzzles. CARE PRIORITY Safe play, appropriate nutrition, and continued veterinary guidance.
LIFE 05 / FORMATION
ADOLESCENCE
6–12 MONTHS
Young cats continue developing physical abilities and individual behavioral preferences. DEVELOPMENT Increasing independence, play complexity, and exploration. ENRICHMENT PROFILE Rotating toys, interactive play, puzzle feeders, climbing structures, and scent exploration. CARE PRIORITY Maintain predictable routines and discuss spaying, neutering, and preventive care with a veterinarian.
LIFE 06 / AUTONOMY
YOUNG ADULT
1–2 YEARS
Adult physical development is generally established. Individual preferences become increasingly apparent. DEVELOPMENT Established movement abilities and behavioral patterns. ENRICHMENT PROFILE Hunting games, food puzzles, climbing, scent trails, interactive play, and environmental variety. CARE PRIORITY Regular veterinary examinations, dental care, and consistent environmental stimulation.
LIFE 07 / STABILITY
MATURE ADULT
3–6 YEARS
Mature cats benefit from stable routines and continued opportunities for exploration. DEVELOPMENT Established adult behavior and individual activity patterns. ENRICHMENT PROFILE Rotating enrichment, interactive hunting, elevated resting areas, window observation, and food-based discovery. CARE PRIORITY Maintain healthy activity, weight monitoring, and preventive veterinary care.
LIFE 08 / TRANSITION
SENIOR
7–10 YEARS
Changes in mobility, sensory ability, and health may begin to influence daily activity. DEVELOPMENT Individual aging patterns and potential changes in mobility. ENRICHMENT PROFILE Low-impact play, accessible resting areas, gentle puzzles, familiar scents, and shorter interactive sessions. CARE PRIORITY Monitor behavioral changes and schedule appropriate veterinary examinations.
LIFE 09 / CONTINUITY
SUPER SENIOR
11+ YEARS
Older cats may benefit from additional environmental accessibility and individualized support. DEVELOPMENT Aging-related changes vary considerably between individuals. ENRICHMENT PROFILE Comfortable bedding, accessible food and water, gentle sensory activities, familiar interaction, and low-impact play. CARE PRIORITY Prioritize comfort, veterinary monitoring, mobility support, and quality of life.

THE ENRICHMENT FIELD MANUAL

COMPANION CARE / RESPONSIBLE PLAY / ENVIRONMENTAL SAFETY

CARE PROTOCOL IX-C001

THE FIVE ENRICHMENT DOMAINS

01 / PREDATORY PLAY

Wand toys, supervised chase games, and simulated hunting activities allow cats to express natural play behaviors.

02 / ENVIRONMENTAL EXPLORATION

Cat-safe tunnels, boxes, shelves, hiding places, and observation areas provide opportunities for discovery.

03 / FOOD DISCOVERY

Appropriate food puzzles and supervised foraging activities can introduce variety to feeding routines.

04 / SENSORY STIMULATION

Safe textures, familiar scents, visual observation, and gentle environmental changes support exploration.

05 / SOCIAL CONNECTION

Respectful interaction, companionship, and opportunities to choose contact support positive social experiences.

SAFETY NOTICE IX-S001

RESPONSIBLE ENRICHMENT

NEVER FORCE PARTICIPATION.
INSPECT TOYS FOR DAMAGE AND SMALL DETACHABLE PARTS.
SUPERVISE STRING, RIBBON, AND WAND TOYS.
AVOID TOXIC PLANTS AND UNSAFE SCENTS.
PROVIDE ACCESS TO FRESH WATER AND RESTING SPACES.
ADAPT ACTIVITIES TO MOBILITY AND HEALTH NEEDS.
CONSULT A VETERINARIAN WHEN HEALTH CONDITIONS AFFECT ACTIVITY OR BEHAVIOR.
